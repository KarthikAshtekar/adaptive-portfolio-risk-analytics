What and why

From strategy idea to evidence

The project addresses a common portfolio-research failure: comparing methods under different data, hidden assumptions, and incomplete cost/risk reporting. It builds one daily-return pipeline in which strategy, covariance, rebalance, costs, defensive exposure, and validation settings are explicit.

System design

Pipeline architecture

Weights selected at date t apply to returns at t+1. All decision-facing regimes and sentiment signals are lagged. Net and gross performance are retained separately.

1. InputsYahoo market data, governed local text, optional provider APIs
2. Research dataInspection, missingness policy, returns, covariance, correlation
3. PortfolioClustering, fixed allocation, regimes, adaptive defensive policy
4. EvidenceRolling backtest, costs, risk, stress, sensitivity, CPCV
5. DeliveryEvidence gates, three dashboard modes, reports and artifacts

Current code is organized by responsibility under src/. Historical stage and bootstrap documents were moved to docs/archive/ so they no longer masquerade as current architecture.

Inputs and quality

Data sourcing and preprocessing

Market data

YahooFinanceProvider requests adjusted close when available, falls back to close, retains volume, normalizes dates, and reports missing observations. The dashboard supports presets and custom Yahoo symbols.

Centralized cleaning

Missingness, interpolation, anomaly handling, return construction, and winsorization live in src/data_pipeline/preprocess.py. Dropped-asset diagnostics explain why and where missingness occurred.

Text data

RBI, earnings, and news inputs use manifests, provenance flags, publication timing, deduplication, source quality, and placeholder exclusion. Private/raw corpora and caches are ignored by Git.

Feature boundary

Portfolio and regime features are implemented. The older generic technical/macro FeatureEngineer is partial and is not presented as a complete factor library.

Portfolio construction

Covariance, clustering, and strategy logic

Covariance choices

  • Sample: direct history, transparent but noisy.
  • Ledoit-Wolf: shrinkage for better conditioning.
  • EWMA: more weight on recent observations.
  • EWMA + Ledoit-Wolf: recency plus shrinkage.

Hierarchy

Correlation becomes a distance matrix, then a linkage tree. HRP uses quasi-diagonal ordering and cluster-variance recursive bisection. HERC follows the actual tree branches and allocates equal risk budgets between each left/right split.

Why HRP and HERC differ: both use the same dependence structure, but they propagate risk budgets differently. A fair comparison holds covariance method, linkage, universe, and date window constant.

Realism and risk

Backtesting, costs, and analytics

Rebalancing

Calendar, threshold, and calendar-or-threshold modes are implemented. Targets update on a configured schedule; threshold triggers compare naturally drifted weights with the latest stored target.

Trading frictions

Turnover is half the absolute weight change. Base cost and slippage are charged at rebalance, with reason, drift, turnover, and transaction cost logged.

Net/gross contract

Net returns reconcile to net final value; gross returns reconcile to gross final value. Cost drag is reported explicitly rather than hidden in one summary number.

Risk metrics

CAGR, volatility, Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, maximum drawdown, Pain Index/Pain Ratio, VaR/ES, concentration, tracking error, information ratio, beta, Jensen's alpha, risk contribution, liquidity, and stress scenarios are implemented.

VaR convention: experiment VaR/CVaR are signed return-tail values. Dashboard historical VaR/ES are positive losses. Compare like with like.
Regimes and robustness

Adaptive allocation and validation

Lag-safe decisions

Rule-based regimes use volatility, drawdown, trend, momentum, correlation, and shocks. HMM walk-forward trains only on prior expanding history. Full-sample HMM is blocked from trading-safe adaptive claims.

Explicit policy map

Each regime selects allocator, covariance method, target volatility, rebalance rule, defensive floor, and risky cap. Conservative, Balanced, and Aggressive presets are transparent transformations.

Sensitivity

Grids vary implemented settings and rank by exactly one selected objective. Turnover and cost are diagnostics unless they alter that objective.

CPCV-style robustness

Ordered time blocks, test combinations, purge, and embargo feed fold medians, worst folds, stability, and failures. The method is useful but does not guarantee out-of-sample success.

Governed monitoring

Sentiment and NLP boundary

The package supports local/provider records, RBI document processing, source quality, ex-ante filters, publication lag, lexicon scoring, optional local FinBERT, composite source mix, and shadow overlays. These outputs are deliberately separated from active strategy scoring and portfolio weights.

Developer View

Inspect raw configs, gate traces, source mix, look-ahead diagnostics, weight history, and net/gross reconciliation.

  1. Choose an asset universe and sufficiently long date range.
  2. Set strategy, covariance, rebalance, target update frequency, and costs.
  3. Run the portfolio and compare net with gross results.
  4. Read risk, stress, trading activity, and concentration before return ranking.
  5. Use sensitivity/CPCV to challenge the chosen configuration.

Material limitations

  • Historical samples and source revisions limit generalization.
  • Adaptive CPCV loses folds to warm-up and training requirements.
  • HMM and defensive-sleeve assumptions are sample-sensitive.
  • Transaction costs omit calibrated nonlinear capacity/impact.
  • NLP history is short and shadow results are mixed.
  • The Streamlit app remains monolithic and lightly browser-tested.
Defensible discussion

Interview defense

Why HERC?

It uses the dependency tree to allocate equal risk budgets across cluster branches. In the matched primary scenario it delivered the strongest growth/terminal value, but not the smallest drawdown.

Why not replace HERC with adaptive?

Adaptive policies improved downside efficiency but sacrificed CAGR and terminal wealth. The defensible conclusion is role separation, not winner replacement.

How is look-ahead controlled?

Weights apply one day later, rule-based observations are lagged, HMM walk-forward trains on prior history, and text availability/publication lags are explicit.

What does CPCV add?

It challenges one historical path through block combinations, purge, embargo, worst-fold behavior, and stability. Failed folds remain part of the conclusion.

Why is NLP not active?

Coverage history is short and the shadow overlay improved some return-efficiency metrics while worsening Pain Index and maximum drawdown versus HMM Conservative.

What would be next?

Broader replication, fold-coverage eligibility, versioned data, calibrated market impact, modular dashboard tests, and longer real NLP history.

Final conclusion: the project is a broad, tested research platform with honest evidence boundaries. It is portfolio-ready as an academic/quant research demonstration, not production-ready trading infrastructure.
